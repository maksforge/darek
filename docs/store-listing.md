# Play store listing copy

**This document is the only copy of the listing text.** The Console is where it is pasted, not where
it lives — a value that exists only in a web form cannot be reviewed, translated as a set, or
recovered when someone edits it by accident.

Three scripts read this file, so its headings are load-bearing:

| Script | Reads |
| --- | --- |
| `scripts/play-whatsnew.py` | the `## Release notes` section, rendering every locale's note in the one format the Console's textarea accepts |
| `scripts/notes-gate.py` | the newest `### x.y.z` heading, refusing a merge where it does not match `versionName` |
| `scripts/play-metadata.py` | the whole thing, generating the `fastlane/metadata/` tree the publish workflow uploads |

> **The gate exists because of a real failure.** A release shipped to production carrying the
> *previous* version's release notes: `versionName` had been bumped and the notes had not, nothing
> compared the two, and Play does not allow release notes to be edited on a live release. The
> correction had to wait for the next upload. Renaming a heading is cheaper.

### Full description — 2869/4000

**No health or safety claims**, here or on any page the listing links to — Play's enforcement reads a
linked page as part of the listing. Say what the app cannot do instead.

The shape the apps built from this template converged on, with headings in capitals because Play
renders no markup. The block below is what `play-metadata.py` uploads, so the outline stays out of it:

1. One or two sentences: the problem, and what the app does about it.
2. `HOW IT WORKS` — what happens, in the order the user meets it.
3. `FREE, AND NOTHING LEAVES YOUR PHONE` — no ads, no account, no server; "it does not request the
   internet permission" only while `aab-permissions.py` forbids `INTERNET`; what it stores.
4. `WHAT IT CANNOT DO` — the honest limits, and what a phone or the platform can stop.
5. `PERMISSIONS` — one line each, in the user's words.
6. A last line pointing at the source: `github.com/maksforge/darek`.

The second paragraph says what the current test build can do. Each test build rewrites it, and the
release that ships the call (phase 3) deletes it, in both languages.

```
Darek is a Polish voice assistant for your gate. Say "Darek, otwórz bramę" and he answers out loud and calls your gate's GSM opener, so you do not have to reach for your phone. Recognition, speech and the call all happen on your phone, with no internet at all.

This test build answers one Command, "Hej Darek, powiedz coś", while the app is open on screen. Listening with the screen off and the call arrive in the next test builds.

HOW IT WORKS

Darek knows a few fixed Commands: for a gate, a garage and a wicket. Give each one the phone number of its opener's SIM, and switch on the ones you use.

You start Listening yourself, in the app, from its notification or from a Quick Settings tile. It never starts on its own, not even after the phone restarts, and while it is on, a notification says that Darek is listening.

When he hears a Command, Darek says what he is about to do ("Otwieram bramę") and gives you about two seconds to cancel: say "Darek, stop" or "anuluj", or tap Cancel in the notification. Then he calls the number you entered. An opener that knows your number opens the gate.

He listens for the whole Command, not just his name. "Darek" in ordinary conversation does nothing, and neither does "Marek, otwórz bramę".

FREE, AND NOTHING LEAVES YOUR PHONE

No ads. No account. No server. No subscription. Darek does not request the internet permission, so nothing he hears can leave your phone. Speech is recognised on the phone by an offline recogniser that comes with the app, and the sound is processed as it is heard: never recorded, never stored, never sent. Darek keeps only his settings: the numbers you entered, which Commands are on, your theme and your language. An optional tip through Google Play unlocks nothing.

WHAT IT CANNOT DO

Darek calls your gate opener; he does not control the gate. Whether it opens is up to the opener, which has to accept calls from your number. The call goes through your own SIM and mobile operator like any call you make, so it needs signal.

He understands only his fixed Polish Commands, not free speech, and you cannot add phrases of your own. In a noisy place he can miss a Command; say it again.

He answers in his own Polish voice, which comes with the app and sounds the same on every phone; he needs no text-to-speech engine and no voice download.

Listening keeps the microphone on and uses battery while it runs. Some phones, Xiaomi's among them, stop apps in the background; Darek opens the battery and autostart settings for you.

PERMISSIONS

- Microphone. Hears the Commands, only while Listening is on.
- Phone. Calls the number you entered for a Command, after Darek's reply.
- Notifications. Shows that Darek is listening, with a button to stop him.
- Foreground service. Keeps Listening running while the app is closed.

Source available, built in the open: github.com/maksforge/darek
```

## Release notes (Play's "What's new", 500 chars per locale)

**Don't paste these by hand.** The Console takes all languages in one textarea, tagged by locale,
and `scripts/play-whatsnew.py` renders exactly that from the blocks below:

```bash
python3 scripts/play-whatsnew.py                 # newest version here, to stdout
python3 scripts/play-whatsnew.py --version 0.1.0 # a named one
```

It reformats and never retypes, so this document stays the only copy. It exits non-zero if a note is
over 500 characters, or if a language the **app** ships has no note at all — both of which Play
accepts in silence and a user then reads.

The label above each block is **parsed**, not decorative: `**<Language>** — <n>/500:` on a
line of its own, with the fenced note under it. The count is what keeps a note from reaching Play
truncated, and `play-whatsnew.py` refuses a language the app ships and this section does not.

**Write these about what changed for the person reading them.** A schema migration is not a release
note; the feature it made possible is. A release where nothing user-visible changed still needs a
heading — rename it and say so in one line, so the decision is deliberate rather than made by
default.
